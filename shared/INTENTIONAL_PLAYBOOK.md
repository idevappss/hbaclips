# Intentional Reels — Question Playbook
Kept by the Intentional Reels session. Fed into every batch (lib/intentional.js). Rewrite when the filmed/skipped pile teaches something new.
Last updated 2026-09-22.

## What the creator has already told us they love
- Hearted clips are **conviction-first manifestos with no setup**: "I serve an abundant God… the only limit is your imagination", "Thinking small is the most selfish thing", "You have to be obsessed", "I don't have to clock in anymore", "You're not a salesman, you're a cashier", "We talk to AI more than our coaches".
- So the best question gets him to **declare a belief and defend it**, not explain a process. Tactics land when they're inside a belief ("you're a cashier" is a sales tactic inside an identity shift).
- Title taste: number first, contrast, correct a belief, "X, not Y", fragments with periods. No hype words.

## The shape of a question that pulls a reel
1. **Force a side.** "Is X true or is it a lie?" / "What's the rule every doctor follows that you think is wrong?" — he answers with certainty, which is his natural register.
2. **Ask for the moment, not the lesson.** "Where were you standing when…" / "What did you do the next morning?" — gets the story with a number, a month, a name.
3. **Use the audience's exact words back to him.** "A doctor told us 'I'm still full-time with patients, how am I going to do all this?' What do you say to her?" — the reel opens on a real objection.
4. **One answer, not a list.** Never "what are the 5 things". Ask "if you could only keep one…".
5. **Make him pick the number.** "What's the number you'd tell a doc to hit before they hire?" — numbers become titles.
6. **Let him disagree with the room.** Clinicians are taught business is dirty, patients pay later, standardize everything. Invite him to break those rules.
7. **Give him a "Doc," opening.** He calls his audience Doc. Questions phrased as a doctor's worry let him start "Doc, listen…".

## The follow-up is where the reel lives
- If the first answer is general: "Give me the number." / "Name the client." / "What month was that?" / "What did it cost you?"
- If he gives a list: "Which one would you keep if you lost the other two?"
- If he gives a speech: "Say that in one sentence to a chiro who's about to quit."

## Avoid
- Hooks that put invented facts in his mouth ("it was a Tuesday", "I don't read a single resume"). A hook is either a line he has said on record, or a claim the question will make him prove. Anything else is a guess dressed as a quote.
- Two questions on the same client pain in one list. One strong version beats two close ones.
- Questions answered by any coach (generic "how do I get more patients").
- Questions that need a whiteboard or a screen to make sense in 40 seconds.
- Anything already posted or already clipped.
- Leading with Atlas as a product pitch. Atlas works as proof inside a story ("we talk to AI more than our coaches"), not as the subject.

## Filming notes
- Ask them in blocks of 3–4 on one topic; his third answer on a topic is usually the tightest.
- Re-ask the best one at the end, cold: "Say the steak dinner thing again, straight to camera." Second takes are the reel.
- Keep the question off-mic or cut it; the reel opens on his first sentence.
